SHORT DESCRIPTION — JODIT:
India hosts more than 58,000 international students from 173 countries across one of the largest higher education systems in the world, with over 64,000 registered higher education institutions and a total enrolment of about 4.5 crore students. For international applicants the case is built on three things: almost all degree teaching at the institutions that recruit internationally is delivered in English, so no second language has to be acquired before or during the degree; published tuition for foreign nationals sits far below comparable destinations, with the University of Delhi charging foreign students the same tuition as Indian students and the Indian Institute of Science charging international undergraduates the same rate as general candidates; and the Government of India funds a large, openly published scholarship programme through the Indian Council for Cultural Relations, which allocated 4,541 fully funded slots across eighteen schemes for the 2026-27 academic year alone. The student visa is generous by international standards, granted for up to five years or the duration of the course, whichever is less. The trade-off is equally clear and is set out in full on this page: an Indian student visa is issued for full-time study and nothing else, there is no permission to work part-time while studying, and there is no general post-study work visa at the end of the course.

OVERVIEW — JODIT:
Why students choose India

India runs one of the largest higher education systems on earth. The All India Survey on Higher Education for 2023-24 recorded 64,756 registered higher education institutions and a total enrolment of roughly 4.50 crore students, up 31.5 per cent in a decade. Within that scale sits a small group of globally ranked institutions: in the QS World University Rankings 2027, published in June 2026, the Indian Institute of Technology Delhi placed 118th, IIT Bombay 134th, IIT Madras 170th, the Indian Institute of Science 221st and the University of Delhi 322nd. IIT Delhi's 118th place is the highest global position an Indian institution has achieved in these rankings.

The international community is real and growing

India hosted 58,134 international students in 2023-24, drawn from 173 countries, up from 48,898 in 2019-20. Nepal is by some distance the largest source country at 24.1 per cent of all foreign enrolments, followed by the United Arab Emirates at 7.0 per cent, the United States at 5.9 per cent, Bangladesh at 5.9 per cent, Nigeria at 5.5 per cent and Zimbabwe at 4.0 per cent. Karnataka is the single largest host state with 7,914 international students, narrowly ahead of Punjab at 7,902, followed by Maharashtra at 6,190, Uttar Pradesh at 5,953 and Tamil Nadu at 5,694.

What it costs

This is where India separates itself from most study destinations. At the University of Delhi, a foreign national's tuition fee is the same as an Indian student's, with total tuition and other fees typically falling between INR 25,000 and INR 60,000 depending on the programme, plus a non-refundable application fee of INR 1,500. At the Indian Institute of Science, international undergraduates on the Bachelor of Science (Research) programme pay the same rate as general candidates, set at INR 80,000 per annum from the 2026-27 academic year. IIT Delhi does charge foreign nationals a distinct rate: for the 2025 entry B.Tech and Dual Degree cohort the institute fee is USD 1,000 plus INR 31,000 for students from SAARC countries, and USD 2,000 plus INR 31,000 for students from non-SAARC countries. Even at the highest of these rates the annual cost sits well below equivalent programmes in the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada or Ireland.

Government scholarships are substantial and openly published

The Indian Council for Cultural Relations administers eighteen scholarship schemes for the 2026-27 academic year, with 4,541 slots in total, funded variously by ICCR itself, the Ministry of External Affairs and the Ministry of Ayush. The largest single scheme is the India-Africa Maitri Scholarship Scheme, with 1,503 slots across 54 African countries. The broadest by geography is the Atal Bihari Vajpayee General Scholarship Scheme, with 554 slots spread across 116 countries. These are not tuition discounts. A typical ICCR award covers the tuition fee paid directly to the university, a monthly stipend, house rent allowance, contingent allowances, a thesis or dissertation allowance, and return economy class airfare to the airport nearest the place of study. Separately, the Ministry of Education runs the Study in India programme, which coordinates admission and fee waivers across a large network of participating institutes.

The visa is generous, and specific

An Indian student visa may be granted for a maximum period of up to five years, or for the duration of the academic course, whichever is less. There is no restriction on the number of courses a foreign student may undertake, and each new course restarts the validity calculation, subject to an overall student visa band that cannot exceed five years. If you do not yet hold a firm admission letter, Indian Missions abroad can issue a Provisional Student Visa valid for six months, which the local FRRO or FRO can convert into a regular Student Visa once you secure confirmed admission. Students holding a visa valid for more than 180 days must register with the local FRRO or FRO within fourteen days of arrival.

What India does not offer, stated plainly

Two things are missing from the Indian student route that exist in most Western destinations, and no amount of marketing changes them. First, the Indian student visa is issued on the express condition that the sole objective of the applicant is to pursue regular and full-time academic studies in India and no other purpose or activity. There is no general concession permitting international students to work part-time during term. Second, India has no general post-study work visa comparable to the United Kingdom's Graduate Route or Ireland's Third Level Graduate Programme. A graduate who wishes to remain and work must obtain an Employment Visa in their own right, which carries its own eligibility conditions and salary thresholds. Plan on the assumption that you will study in India and then leave, unless and until you have a job offer that independently qualifies for an Employment Visa.

Who India suits

India suits the student who wants a globally ranked engineering, science, management or humanities degree taught in English at a fraction of Western tuition, who is a candidate for one of the government scholarship schemes, who has a specific academic or research reason to be in India, or who intends to return home or to a third country after graduating. It suits less well the student whose primary objective is migration, or who needs part-time earnings to fund their studies. Both of those are structural features of the system rather than obstacles that better planning can remove.

TUITION NOTES — JODIT:
All figures below are the rates published by each institution for the academic year stated. Indian institutions set fees individually and revise them annually. Verify on the institution's own fees page before you commit money.

The structural point about Indian tuition

At several leading Indian institutions a foreign national pays the same tuition as an Indian national. This is unusual and it is the single largest reason the total cost of an Indian degree sits so far below equivalent programmes in the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia or Canada.

University of Delhi
A foreign national student's tuition fee is the same as that of an Indian student. Tuition and other fees vary between INR 25,000 and INR 60,000 depending on the programme of study. Students from SAARC countries and Tibetan nationals pay the same fees as Indian students. A non-refundable application fee of INR 1,500 applies at the Foreign Students' Registry, and registration fees are payable both at the FSR and at the allotted college or department. All fees are paid in Indian Rupees by online transfer; cash is not accepted at any stage of admission.

Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru
For the undergraduate Bachelor of Science (Research) programme, international students pay the same rate as general candidates. Tuition for that programme is INR 80,000 per annum from the academic year 2026-27. International research students receive a fellowship equal to that offered to Indian students, which IISc states is usually adequate to cover tuition, hostel and mess fees and living expenses.

Indian Institute of Technology Delhi
IIT Delhi does charge foreign nationals a distinct institute fee. For the 2025 entry B.Tech and Dual Degree cohort:
SAARC countries — USD 1,000 tuition plus INR 22,400 other charges plus INR 8,600 institute residence fee, giving USD 1,000 plus INR 31,000.
Non-SAARC countries — USD 2,000 tuition plus INR 22,400 other charges plus INR 8,600 institute residence fee, giving USD 2,000 plus INR 31,000.
For comparison, an Indian national in the General, OBC-NCL or EWS category with annual family income above INR 5 lakh pays INR 1,00,000 tuition plus other charges, with a total institute fee of INR 1,31,000 with institute residence allotted.

What tuition does not include
Hostel and private accommodation charges, mess charges, security deposits and caution money are payable separately at every institution in this guide. So are fees for obtaining certificates, and registration fees or penalties relating to registration and updating with immigration authorities. The University of Delhi additionally requires admitted foreign students to hold medical insurance worth INR 5,00,000 and to produce proof of financial guarantee and a No Objection Certificate from their embassy in India at document verification.

A note on dollar-denominated fees
Where an institute quotes part of its fee in US dollars, as IIT Delhi does, the rupee equivalent moves with the exchange rate. Budget against the dollar figure, not against a rupee conversion published on any third-party site.

LIVING COST NOTES — JODIT:
There is no single official national figure

Unlike several other study destinations, the Government of India does not publish a single national living-cost figure that international students must meet. What the Ministry of Home Affairs requires instead is evidence, to the satisfaction of the Indian Mission or Post, of the transfer of adequate funds for at least four months of sustenance in India, or travellers cheques for a similar amount, together with proof that the applicant is a person of assured financial standing. A letter of support from a parent or guardian accompanied by a bank guarantee or certificate may be accepted for this purpose.

What that means in practice
The four-month sustenance test is assessed by the Mission against your specific city, institution and circumstances. It is not a published number you can pre-compute. Prepare bank statements, a parental support letter and a bank guarantee, and expect the Mission to make a judgement on them.

Costs vary enormously by city
India is not a single cost environment. Metropolitan Mumbai, New Delhi and Bengaluru carry materially higher accommodation costs than smaller university cities, and within each city the difference between an institute hostel place and a private rental is substantial. Institute hostel accommodation at the institutions in this guide is charged separately from tuition and is generally the lowest-cost option available.

What you must budget for beyond tuition
Hostel or private accommodation charges. Mess charges. A refundable security deposit and caution money. Medical insurance — the University of Delhi requires cover worth INR 5,00,000 for admitted foreign students. Fees for obtaining certificates. FRRO or FRO registration fees, and any penalties if registration or updating is late.

Do not plan to fund living costs from work
This matters more in India than in most destinations. An Indian student visa is granted on the condition that the sole objective of the applicant is regular, full-time academic study and no other purpose or activity. There is no general concession allowing international students to work part-time during term. Your funds must cover your stay without employment income.

VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING: obtain current city-level living-cost guidance from each institution's international office before publishing any specific rupee figure on the public page.

PART-TIME WORK SUMMARY — JODIT:
The Indian Student Visa does not carry a right to work

This is the clearest structural difference between studying in India and studying in the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia or Canada, and it is stated as an eligibility condition rather than buried in guidance. The Ministry of Home Affairs sets out that the sole objective of the applicant should be to pursue regular and full-time academic studies in India and no other purpose or activity.

There is no general concession comparable to Ireland's twenty hours a week on Stamp 2 or the United Kingdom's twenty hours on a Student visa. Do not plan your finances on the assumption that you will earn while studying.

What is permitted
Where your course itself requires industrial training, a summer project or an internship as part of the prescribed curriculum, no special permission is required to complete it. That is a curriculum requirement rather than a work right, and it does not extend to employment outside the programme.

What this means for your application
Because employment income is not available to you, the financial evidence you present at the visa stage carries more weight than it would elsewhere. The Mission expects proof of assured financial standing and evidence of transfer of adequate funds for at least four months of sustenance in India. Assemble that evidence properly rather than assuming part-time work will fill a gap.

POST-STUDY WORK SUMMARY — JODIT:
India has no general post-study work visa

There is no Indian equivalent of the United Kingdom's Graduate Route, Ireland's Third Level Graduate Programme Stamp 1G, Australia's Temporary Graduate visa or Canada's Post-Graduation Work Permit. A Student Visa is issued for the duration of the course, and it does not convert into a work permission when the course ends.

What happens at the end of your course
Your Student Visa is tied to your programme. When the programme finishes, the permission finishes with it. A foreign student who discontinues a course abruptly and voluntarily seeks exit from India may have their Student Visa cancelled by the FRRO or FRO without prejudice, and an exit permit is issued on production of a valid passport and visa and a no-dues certificate from the institution.

If you want to stay and work
You must qualify for an Employment Visa in your own right, on the basis of a job offer from an Indian employer. That is a separate visa category with its own eligibility conditions, and it is not granted as a continuation of student status.

If you want to keep studying
This is where India is unusually flexible. There is no restriction on the number of courses a foreign student may undertake, and each time you start a new course the period of validity of the Student Visa is counted afresh — subject to the overall student visa band, which cannot exceed five years. If a course you are undertaking requires a stay beyond five years from the initial validity of the visa, you must obtain a visa extension from the jurisdictional FRRO or FRO. Changing course midway is permitted with the permission of the local FRRO or FRO and the institutions concerned, and the validity is then recalculated against the new course.

Plan accordingly
Treat an Indian degree as a degree, not as a migration route. If your objective is to work in India afterwards, research the Employment Visa requirements before you apply for the Student Visa, not after you graduate.

IMMIGRATION PATHWAY SUMMARY — JODIT:
There is no student-to-settlement pathway

Several study destinations connect a student permission to a graduate work permission and then to settlement. India does not. The Student Visa is a study permission with a defined end, and nothing in the student route leads onward.

The route to working in India
A foreign national who wants to work in India applies for an Employment Visa on the basis of a job offer from an Indian employer. It is assessed on its own terms and is not a continuation of, or an upgrade from, student status. VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING: the Employment Visa carries eligibility conditions including a minimum annual remuneration threshold. Confirm the current threshold and the current list of exemptions directly with the Bureau of Immigration at boi.gov.in before publishing any figure.

What the student route does allow
Within the student category itself, India is flexible. Up to five years of Student Visa validity. No cap on the number of courses. Course and institution changes permitted with FRRO or FRO approval, within fifteen days where the transfer is within the same FRRO jurisdiction and within sixty days where it crosses jurisdictions. Extensions available where a student fails to qualify within the prescribed time, as long as the institution permits continuation, with a normal expectation that a course is completed within three attempts.

Family
The Ministry of Home Affairs states expressly that parents and family members of a student will not be granted a long-term visa for India on the ground that their child is a student in India. For Bangladeshi nationals there is a narrow exception: spouses and dependent children of postgraduate students only may be eligible for an Entry (X) visa for the same period, on proof of adequate financial support.

Be realistic in your planning
If your objective is permanent migration, India is not the destination for it through the student route. If your objective is a strong, low-cost degree that you will take back home or to a third country, the absence of a settlement pathway costs you nothing.

VISA PROCESS — JODIT:
Step 1 — Check you meet the eligibility conditions

The Ministry of Home Affairs sets five conditions for a Student Visa:
The sole objective of the applicant should be to pursue regular and full-time academic studies in India, and no other purpose or activity.
The applicant should hold a valid passport, and a re-entry permit if the law of their country of nationality requires one.
The applicant should produce proof of admission to a full-time course at a recognised or reputed educational institution in India.
The applicant should produce a letter of approval or No Objection Certificate from the Ministry of Health, in the case of admission to a medical or para-medical course.
The applicant should be a person of assured financial standing. A letter of support from a parent or guardian accompanied by a bank guarantee or certificate may be accepted for this purpose.

Step 2 — Assemble financial evidence
You must produce evidence of transfer of adequate funds for at least four months of sustenance in India, or travellers cheques for a similar amount, to the satisfaction of the Mission or Post.

Step 3 — Apply to the Indian Mission, or take a Provisional Student Visa
If you already hold confirmed admission to a reputed or recognised institute, you can obtain a Student Visa directly from an Indian Mission abroad.

If you do not yet have a firm letter of admission — because the institution requires an admission test, for example — the Mission or Post may grant a Provisional Student Visa on the basis of a provisional admission certificate, a letter of offer, or an advertisement for admission to foreign students issued by the institution. A Provisional Student Visa is valid for six months and may be extended by the jurisdictional FRRO or FRO for up to six months on satisfactory documentary evidence. No institution name is printed on a Provisional Student Visa, because you may be trying several institutions and alternative courses at once.

Note carefully: you cannot enter India on a Tourist Visa to explore admission and then convert. That route is expressly not permitted. The provisional route above exists for exactly that situation.

Step 4 — Understand your visa validity
A Student Visa may be granted for a maximum period of up to five years, or for the duration of the academic course of study, whichever is less, to nationals of all countries except Pakistan, Bangladesh and China, for whom separate provisions apply.

VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING: the Ministry of Home Affairs overview states that a maximum of three entries per academic year may be allowed to facilitate home visits during vacation, while the Ministry's own Student Visa FAQ states that normally one entry is allowed, with additional entries available on bona fide grounds through the Visa Facilitation Centre, Foreigners Division, Ministry of Home Affairs, New Delhi. Confirm the current position before publishing either figure.

Step 5 — Register on arrival
A foreign national entering India on a Student Visa valid for more than 180 days must register with the jurisdictional FRRO or FRO within fourteen days of arrival. Pakistani nationals must register within twenty-four hours.

At provisional registration, produce your valid passport and visa, a bona fide student certificate from the institution, and a provisional address issued by the Head of Department, Principal or Director of the International Studies Centre of the institution.

Final registration must be completed within ninety days of arrival, on production of proof of permanent residence — which includes regular hostel accommodation — and documentary evidence of financial support adequate to sustain you during your studies.

Step 6 — Keep the permission current
Registration and renewal of residential permits are normally completed by the FRRO or FRO within one day of a complete application, after security checks. Requests for an exit permit are issued preferably the same day and in any case within three days, on production of a valid passport and visa and a no-dues certificate from the institution. Transfers between institutions are cleared within fifteen days within the same FRRO jurisdiction and within sixty days where the transfer crosses jurisdictions, on production of an original cancellation certificate from the existing institution and an admission confirmation certificate from the new one.

Approvals you may or may not need
For admission to a diploma, degree or postgraduate degree in Engineering and Technology, Architecture and Town Planning, Pharmacy, Applied Arts, MBA, MCA, and Hotel Management and Catering Technology, no No Objection Certificate is required from the education ministry. For medical or para-medical courses, a No Objection Certificate from the Ministry of Health is required.

VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING: the Ministry of Home Affairs Student Visa overview used throughout this section carries a revision date of 10 September 2014. An e-Student Visa sub-category also now exists under the e-Visa scheme. Confirm the current procedure, the current sub-categories and the current documentary requirements on boi.gov.in and indianvisaonline.gov.in before publishing this page.

PROOF OF FUNDS SUMMARY — JODIT:
What you must show

Assured financial standing. The applicant should be a person of assured financial standing. A letter of support from a parent or guardian, accompanied by a bank guarantee or certificate, may be accepted for this purpose.

Four months of sustenance. The applicant should produce evidence of transfer of adequate funds for at least four months of sustenance in India, or travellers cheques for a similar amount, to the satisfaction of the Mission or Post.

Again at registration. Final registration with the FRRO or FRO, which must be completed within ninety days of arrival, requires documentary evidence of financial support available to the student sufficient to sustain them adequately during their studies.

There is no published rupee figure
The Government of India does not publish a fixed amount for the four-month sustenance test. It is assessed by the Mission against your circumstances, your city and your institution. Prepare bank statements, a parental or guardian support letter and a bank guarantee, and expect a judgement rather than a checklist.

Institution-level requirements sit on top
The University of Delhi requires admitted foreign students to produce, at document verification, a No Objection Certificate from their embassy in India, medical insurance worth INR 5,00,000, and proof of financial guarantee, alongside original documents with two self-attested copies and two passport-size photographs.

Remember that employment income is not available to you
The Student Visa is granted on the condition that study is your sole objective. There is no part-time work concession. Your financial evidence has to stand on its own.

WAIVER NOTES — JODIT:
The practical position for most applicants

Degree teaching at the institutions that recruit internationally in India is delivered in English. Where an applicant's previous education was itself conducted through the medium of English, many Indian institutions will admit on that basis without requiring a separate standardised English test.

This is a practice rather than a published national rule, and it is applied programme by programme. It is not a formal waiver process with a named form and a published condition set, of the kind operated by universities in Ireland or the United Kingdom.

What to do
Contact the international office of the institution directly, before you apply, and ask two specific questions. First, does this programme require a standardised English test for a candidate of my nationality and educational background. Second, if my previous degree was taught in English, what documentary evidence do you require in place of a test.

Get the answer in writing and keep it with your application file. If the institution confirms no test is needed, that confirmation is what your file needs.

VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING: confirm the waiver position at each institution before publishing this as general guidance.

GENERAL NOTES — JODIT:
Why this section carries no scores

Every other field on this card has been left blank deliberately. India does not operate a national English language requirement for international students, Indian institutions set their own admission requirements independently and per programme, and publishing an invented national minimum would be actively misleading to an applicant.

What is true across the board
English is one of the two languages in which the Government of India conducts official business, and it is the language of instruction for essentially all degree programmes at the institutions that recruit international students. An international student does not need Hindi to study in India, although some knowledge makes daily life considerably easier outside the campus.

What varies
Whether a standardised English test is required at all. Which tests are accepted. What the minimum score is where one applies. Whether prior English-medium education is accepted in place of a test, and what evidence is required for that.

How to establish your own requirement
Identify the exact programme, not the institution. Open its admission page. Contact the international office or, at the University of Delhi, the Foreign Students' Registry, and ask about your specific nationality and educational background. Where you are applying through a government scheme, ask the scheme administrator as well — the Indian Council for Cultural Relations handles both admission and scholarship through its Admissions to Alumni portal, and its guidance will state what it requires.

Do not sit an expensive test before you have established that it is needed. Equally, do not assume it is not needed because a general page did not mention it.

### FAQ 1

QUESTION:
How much does it cost to study in India as an international student?

ANSWER — JODIT:
Less than almost any comparable destination, and at several leading institutions a foreign national pays exactly what an Indian national pays.

University of Delhi. A foreign national student's tuition fee is the same as that of an Indian student. Tuition and other fees vary between INR 25,000 and INR 60,000 depending on the programme. A non-refundable application fee of INR 1,500 applies at the Foreign Students' Registry, and registration fees are payable at the FSR and again at the allotted college or department. Students from SAARC countries and Tibetan nationals pay the same fees as Indian students.

Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru. International undergraduates on the Bachelor of Science (Research) programme pay the same rate as general candidates, set at INR 80,000 per annum from the academic year 2026-27.

Indian Institute of Technology Delhi. A distinct foreign national rate does apply. For the 2025 entry B.Tech and Dual Degree cohort the institute fee is USD 1,000 plus INR 31,000 for SAARC country students, and USD 2,000 plus INR 31,000 for non-SAARC country students.

On top of tuition you must budget for hostel or private accommodation, mess charges, a security deposit and caution money, certificate fees, FRRO or FRO registration fees, and medical insurance — the University of Delhi requires cover worth INR 5,00,000.

### FAQ 2

QUESTION:
Can I work part-time while studying in India?

ANSWER — JODIT:
No. This is the single most important thing to understand before choosing India, and it is stated as a condition of eligibility rather than buried in guidance.

The Ministry of Home Affairs sets out that the sole objective of the applicant should be to pursue regular and full-time academic studies in India, and no other purpose or activity. There is no general concession permitting international students to work part-time during term, and nothing comparable to the twenty hours a week available on a UK Student visa or on Ireland's Stamp 2.

What is permitted. Where your course itself requires industrial training, a summer project or an internship as part of the prescribed curriculum, no special permission is required to complete it. That is a curriculum requirement, not a work right, and it does not extend to employment outside your programme.

What this means for your planning. Your funds must cover your entire stay without employment income. It also raises the weight placed on your financial evidence at the visa stage, where you must show assured financial standing and evidence of transfer of adequate funds for at least four months of sustenance in India.

### FAQ 3

QUESTION:
Is there a post-study work visa in India?

ANSWER — JODIT:
No. India does not operate a general post-study work visa. There is no Indian equivalent of the United Kingdom's Graduate Route, Ireland's Third Level Graduate Programme Stamp 1G, Australia's Temporary Graduate visa or Canada's Post-Graduation Work Permit.

A Student Visa is granted for the duration of your course, up to a maximum of five years, and it does not convert into a work permission when the course ends. At the end of your programme an exit permit is issued, where required, on production of your valid passport and visa and a no-dues certificate from your institution.

If you want to stay and work. You must qualify for an Employment Visa in your own right, on the basis of a job offer from an Indian employer. That is a separate visa category assessed on its own terms, and it is not a continuation of or an upgrade from student status. VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING: confirm the current Employment Visa eligibility conditions and remuneration threshold directly with the Bureau of Immigration before publishing any figure.

If you want to keep studying. Here India is unusually flexible. There is no restriction on the number of courses you may undertake, and the validity of your Student Visa is counted afresh each time you begin a new one, within an overall student visa band of five years.

The honest summary. Treat an Indian degree as a degree, not as a migration route. If your objective is to work in India afterwards, research the Employment Visa requirements before you apply for a Student Visa, not after you graduate.

### FAQ 4

QUESTION:
How long is an Indian student visa valid for?

ANSWER — JODIT:
A Student Visa may be granted for a maximum period of up to five years, or for the duration of the academic course of study, whichever is less. This applies to nationals of all countries other than Pakistan, Bangladesh and China, for whom separate provisions apply.

Several features of the Indian student regime are more generous than they first appear:
There is no restriction on the number of courses you may undertake, and each time you begin a new course the period of validity is counted afresh — subject to the overall five-year student visa band.
There is no restriction on the number of institutions, provided each is recognised or reputed.
If you change course midway, validity is recalculated against the duration of the new course.
If a course requires a stay beyond five years from the initial validity of the visa, you must obtain an extension from the jurisdictional FRRO or FRO.
Extensions are available where a student fails to qualify within the prescribed time, as long as the institution permits continuation, with a normal expectation that a course is completed within three attempts.

If you do not yet hold a firm admission letter, an Indian Mission can issue a Provisional Student Visa valid for six months, extendable by the local FRRO or FRO by up to six months, which converts to a regular Student Visa once you secure confirmed admission.

### FAQ 5

QUESTION:
What scholarships are available for international students in India?

ANSWER — JODIT:
India funds one of the more substantial publicly documented scholarship programmes of any study destination.

The Indian Council for Cultural Relations administers eighteen schemes for the 2026-27 academic year, with 4,541 slots in total, funded variously by ICCR, the Ministry of External Affairs and the Ministry of Ayush. The largest schemes by slot count are the India-Africa Maitri Scholarship Scheme with 1,503 slots across 54 African countries, the Special Scholarship Scheme for Afghan Nationals with 1,000 slots, the Atal Bihari Vajpayee General Scholarship Scheme with 554 slots across 116 countries, the Suborno Jayanti Scholarship Scheme for Bangladesh nationals with 500 slots, the Mekong Ganga Cooperation Scholarship Scheme with 235 slots, and Aid to Sri Lanka with 200 slots.

What an ICCR award typically covers. Tuition fee paid directly to the university, a monthly stipend, house rent allowance, contingent allowances, a thesis or dissertation allowance, and return economy class airfare to the airport nearest the place of study, plus three-tier AC train fare within India where required.

What the student pays. Hostel or private accommodation charges, mess charges, security deposit, caution money, fees for obtaining certificates, registration fees or penalties relating to immigration authorities, and anything else not covered by the scheme norms.

Important exclusions. Most ICCR schemes cover UG, PG and PhD levels in both STEM and non-STEM streams, but expressly exclude Medical, Law and Integrated courses.

Separately, the Ministry of Education's Study in India programme coordinates admission and fee waivers across its partner network, with waivers offered at 100 per cent, 50 per cent and 25 per cent levels.

Applications for ICCR schemes are made through the Admissions to Alumni portal, which handles the scholarship application and the university admission together in a single process.

### FAQ 6

QUESTION:
Do I need IELTS or TOEFL to study in India?

ANSWER — JODIT:
Often not, but you must confirm it rather than assume it.

India does not operate a national English language requirement for international students. Indian institutions set their own admission criteria independently and programme by programme, and many will admit on the basis of prior English-medium education without requiring a standardised test.

What is consistent. Degree teaching at the institutions that recruit international students is delivered in English. You do not need Hindi to study in India, although some knowledge makes daily life outside the campus considerably easier.

What varies. Whether a test is required at all. Which tests are accepted. What the minimum score is where one applies. What documentary evidence of English-medium education is accepted in place of a test.

What to do. Identify the exact programme, not just the institution. Contact its international office — or, at the University of Delhi, the Foreign Students' Registry — and ask two specific questions: does this programme require a standardised English test for a candidate of my nationality and educational background, and if not, what evidence of English-medium education do you require instead. Get the answer in writing and keep it in your application file. If you are applying through a government scholarship scheme, ask the scheme administrator the same questions, because their requirements are separate.

Do not sit an expensive test before you have established that it is needed, and do not assume it is unnecessary because a general page did not mention it.

### FAQ 7

QUESTION:
When are the intakes, and when should I start applying?

ANSWER — JODIT:
The main Indian academic year begins in July and August. A smaller January intake exists at some institutions and on some programmes.

At the University of Delhi, classes for bachelor's programmes have started in the last week of August in recent sessions, with the list of UG courses available for foreign nationals typically ready in the first week of July and provisional admission lists published from mid-August. Postgraduate lists follow at the end of August.

Work backwards, and allow more time than you think. You need, in order: an admission decision, then in-person document verification at the institution — at the University of Delhi this must be completed before a college is allotted and before a fee payment link is even generated — then fee payment, then the Student Visa application at the Indian Mission, and then registration with the FRRO or FRO within fourteen days of arrival.

Several of those steps have lead times outside your control. An embassy No Objection Certificate is issued by your own diplomatic mission in India on their timetable. Medical insurance to the sum the institution requires must be purchased before verification. And neither the Bureau of Immigration nor the Ministry of Home Affairs publishes a national Student Visa processing time, so you should ask the Indian Mission in your country directly.

Six to nine months before the intake is comfortable. Three months is not.

### FAQ 8

QUESTION:
What is the FRRO and why does it matter?

ANSWER — JODIT:
The Foreigners Regional Registration Office, and its district-level counterpart the Foreigners Registration Office, is the authority you deal with for everything relating to your stay in India after you arrive. It matters because several hard deadlines run from your arrival date.

Within fourteen days of arrival. If your Student Visa is valid for more than 180 days you must register with the jurisdictional FRRO or FRO. Pakistani nationals must register within twenty-four hours. At provisional registration you produce your valid passport and visa, a bona fide student certificate from your institution, and a provisional address issued by the Head of Department, Principal or Director of the International Studies Centre.

Within ninety days of arrival. Final registration, on production of proof of permanent residence — regular hostel accommodation counts — and documentary evidence of financial support sufficient to sustain you during your studies.

Throughout your stay. The FRRO or FRO grants extensions to your Student Visa, permits changes of course or institution, and issues exit permits. Transfers between institutions are cleared within fifteen days where the transfer stays within the same FRRO jurisdiction and within sixty days where it crosses jurisdictions, on production of an original cancellation certificate from your existing institution and an admission confirmation certificate from the new one.

At the end. Exit permits, where required, are issued preferably on the same day and in any case within three days, on production of a valid passport and visa and a no-dues certificate from your institution.

Ask your international office for your bona fide certificate and provisional address letter in your first week. Both are routine documents for them and both sit on a fourteen-day clock for you.

### FAQ 9

QUESTION:
Can I come to India on a tourist visa and then apply for admission?

ANSWER — JODIT:
No. The Ministry of Home Affairs states expressly that a foreign student cannot come on a Tourist Visa to explore admission and then obtain a Student Visa while in India.

The route that does exist. If you want to explore options before committing, apply for a Provisional Student Visa of six months duration from an Indian Mission abroad. It is granted on the basis of a provisional admission certificate, a letter of offer, or an advertisement for admission to foreign students issued by the institution — you do not need a firm admission letter.

No institution name is printed on a Provisional Student Visa, precisely because you may be attempting several institutions and alternative courses simultaneously.

Once you secure confirmed admission while in India, you apply to the local FRRO or FRO for a regular Student Visa, with documentary evidence of confirmed admission and financial standing. The FRRO or FRO is empowered to grant a regular Student Visa for the duration of the course or five years, whichever is less.

One exclusion. These provisions do not apply to Theological Studies.

### FAQ 10

QUESTION:
Can my family come with me to India while I study?

ANSWER — JODIT:
Generally no, and the Ministry of Home Affairs states this directly rather than leaving it to be inferred.

The parents and family members of a student will not be allowed a long-term visa for India on the ground that their ward or child is a student in India.

The one narrow exception in the published rules concerns Bangladeshi nationals: spouses and dependent children of postgraduate students only may be eligible for an Entry (X) visa for the same period as the student, on production of proof of adequate financial support for the maintenance of the family.

Plan on the basis that you are coming alone. Combined with the absence of any part-time work concession and the absence of a post-study work route, the family position is one of the clearest ways in which the Indian student route differs from the United Kingdom, Ireland, Canada or Australia, and it should be factored into the decision at the start rather than discovered later.

The University of Delhi ranked 322nd in the QS World University Rankings 2027 with an overall score of 43.8. It is a large, collegiate central university spread across colleges and departments in New Delhi, and it teaches an unusually broad catalogue — arts, commerce, sciences, social sciences, law, management and education — which makes it the most realistic Indian destination for an international applicant whose subject is not engineering or pure science.

The Foreign Students' Registry

The FSR is the single window for all foreign nationals who wish to take admission to the University of Delhi in any course. Everything routes through it: application, verification, college allotment and fee payment. That centralisation is a genuine advantage for an international applicant dealing with a collegiate university.

What it costs

A foreign national student's tuition fee is the same as that of an Indian student. Tuition and other fees vary between INR 25,000 and INR 60,000 depending on the programme of study. Students from SAARC countries and Tibetan nationals pay the same fees as Indian students. Foreign nationals do pay registration fees at the FSR and again at the allotted college or department, over and above tuition.

The application itself carries a non-refundable application fee of INR 1,500. All fees are paid in Indian Rupees by online transfer through cards or net banking; no cash is accepted at any stage of the admission process.

How admission actually runs

The sequence matters, because each step gates the next:
One. Register on the FSR online admission portal. Applicants routed through the Study in India portal must fill the FSR form using the UID generated there.
Two. Submit results — bachelor's applicants have been required to send their Class XII result to the FSR verification address by a stated deadline, with failure treated as withdrawal of interest.
Three. Attend online or in-person verification. Selected applicants for bachelor's and master's courses are contacted by email to arrange it.
Four. Attend document verification at the FSR itself — not at the college and not at the department — bringing original documents with two self-attested copies, two passport-size photographs, a No Objection Certificate from your embassy in India, medical insurance worth INR 5,00,000, and proof of financial guarantee.
Five. College allotment is made after verification.
Six. A fee payment link is generated after allotment, and access to classes follows payment.

Timings
The list of undergraduate courses available to foreign nationals has typically been ready in the first week of July, with provisional admission lists published by mid-August and postgraduate lists by the end of August. Bachelor's classes have started in the last week of August. Note that the foreign national timetable is partly dependent on the domestic admission cycle, and in some sessions departmental classes have started late because domestic admissions were still running.

Deadlines that are published separately
Some programmes run their own timetable. VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING: check the FSR site for current-cycle deadlines rather than relying on any date quoted here, as individual programme deadlines are announced and extended on the FSR homepage through the cycle.

# MANUAL TEST NOTES

## Admin to public rendering limitations noticed

1. The public country page renders only four editorial section keys. `apps/web/src/components/reference/CountryDetailReference.tsx` maps exactly four keys — `why-study`, `application-steps`, `cost-of-study` and `visa-process` — and renders only the Eyebrow, the Heading and the RICH_TEXT paragraphs for each. Sections 5 and 6 in this pack use the free-text keys `india-at-a-glance` and `plan-your-india-application` and will not appear on the public country page today. Neither will the FACT_GRID, CARD_GRID, STEPS, CTA or MEDIA body types, nor the Subheading, Primary media, Secondary media, CTA label or CTA URL fields, which that component does not read. Section 3 in this pack uses the live key `application-steps` but the STEPS body type, so its key is routed but its body will not render — that combination is deliberate, to test exactly that gap.

2. The City module has almost no content surface. The City Admin exposes only Country, State/province, City name, Short description and the SEO block. Short description is a single-line input over a VarChar(1000) column. There is no Overview field in the Admin, no rich text, no media picker, no cost fields and no content sections. The `overview` column exists on `model City` but nothing in the City screen writes to it. The public city page at `apps/web/src/app/study-in/[countrySlug]/[citySlug]/page.tsx` reads only name, shortDescription and country. City pages therefore cannot be made to look populated with the current schema.

3. No Indian intake existed in the global Intakes module. The three existing records are January-March, September and May. India's main academic intake is July and August, so section 6A of this pack creates a July-August Intake record first. Every course and offering in the pack depends on it.

4. The Scholarship Provider dropdown had nothing in it. The `scholarship_providers` table was empty, so section 8 must be entered before section 9.

5. The consultant Locations multi-select has no options. The `consultant_locations` table is empty, so that field cannot be populated on the consultant record.

6. The Country form's Currency selectors appear in the order Currency name, Currency code, Currency symbol. The symbol dropdown renders its options as "₹ · INR" rather than as a bare symbol.

7. Forty orphaned City records exist. All 40 cities currently in the database have a `country_id` pointing at Country rows that no longer exist. None of them is an Indian city, so nothing in this pack collides with them, but they will appear in city listings and filters alongside the three new Indian cities.

8. Two junk Subject drafts exist. `aRTS` (slug `arts`) and `cse` (slug `cse`) are both DRAFT and both pre-date this pack. Neither is used here. `cse` duplicates the Computer Science concept.

9. One junk Country feature exists. A feature named `uqekudwk` with code `UQEKUDWK` sits alongside the eight system features in the global taxonomy.
